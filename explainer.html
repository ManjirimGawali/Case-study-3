How the Daily FC P&L Monitor works

What problem it solves, how each part works, the architecture, a worked example from the real sample, what to practise, and questions with answers.

1Purpose

Leadership needs to know, on the same day, when a fulfilment centre's margin slips and which cost line caused it. Then the right person, the FC manager, needs to be told in a way they can act on in 20 seconds.

Compute

Every FC, every day: each cost as % of revenue, CM1, CM1%, CM2, CM2%.

Judge

Flag lines outside target, margins more than ±3 pp off target, and colour the day Red, Yellow, Green or Blue.

Explain

One plain sentence: how far off, which lines, the biggest ₹ contributors, and what to do.

Deliver

An email digest per FC manager, a leadership roll-up, and a dashboard across all FCs.

3Key ideas

CM1  = Revenue − Manpower − Packaging                 (direct, volume-linked costs)
CM2  = CM1 − Power&Fuel − FC Rent − Equipment − Overheads  (after site costs)
CM1% = CM1 / Revenue × 100      targets: CM1 65–70%, CM2 15–30%, flag beyond ±3 pp
ColourCM2%Meaning
RED< 12Margin breach. Act today

Each day also gets a class: Clean, Line-item-only breach (margins fine, a line drifted), CM1 breach, CM2 breach, Suspiciously high CM2, or Data issue.

4Architecture

5One run, step by step

  1. Upload returns at once with a run id. The browser polls progress, so you can close the tab.
  2. Ingest sniffs the file type, finds the header row by synonyms, works out the FC (column, sheet, or typed name), turns "Day 27" into 2026-05-27, parses ₹1,23,456 and (1,200), and lists every bad row as a data issue. It also detects a target table inside the workbook.
  3. Compute works out % of revenue, CM1 and CM2 for each FC-day.
  4. Rules produce the anomaly rows (Date | FC | Line | % | Target | Status, plus deviation, ₹ impact and severity), the colour and the class.
  5. Analyst loads the FC's history (this file plus memory), finds the 14-day median "normal", ranks breaches by ₹, splits each into a cost or revenue effect, looks for ₹0 or unusually low lines on Blue days and for 4-day trends, recalls past explanations, and picks an action from the playbook. The result is a facts object and a template sentence.
  6. Writer (optional) asks a local model to rephrase the sentence and keeps the result only if every number and line survives.
  7. Digest builds one email per FC: headline insight, P&L table, top issues, "Watch" trends, a 7-day colour strip and a dashboard link. It also builds a leadership roll-up.
  8. Dispatcher writes .html and .eml files to the outbox, sends via SMTP (Mailpit or Gmail) and Slack, and records the result for each manager.

6Worked example: the provided sample's worst day

Day 69 (Jul 8). Revenue ₹91,879; Manpower ₹27,943, Packaging ₹7,554, Power & Fuel ₹12,464, FC Rent ₹18,315, Equipment ₹4,346, Overheads ₹12,452.

CM1 = 91,879 − 27,943 − 7,554           = 56,382  → 61.37%  (below 62 → CM1 flag)
CM2 = 56,382 − 12,464 − 18,315 − 4,346 − 12,452 = 8,805 → 9.58%   (below 12 → RED, CM2 flag)
FC Rent  19.93% vs max 18% → +1.93 pp → ₹1,777 impact     Overheads 13.55% → ₹1,427 · Packaging 8.22% → ₹1,122 · …

Cause split for rent. "Normal" (the 14-day median) is ₹16.6k of rent on ₹1.14L of revenue (14.6%). Today rent is ₹18.3k (+10%), but revenue is only ₹91.9k (−19%). Cost effect = (18,315 − 16,624) / 91,879 = +1.8 pp; revenue effect = 16,624/91,879 − 16,624/113,579 = +3.5 pp. Revenue is the larger part, so the cause is revenue, and the text says "mainly low revenue, amount +10.2% vs normal". Across the whole day, total cost moved −4.8% while revenue fell 19.1%, so the recommended action is about volume, not cost cutting.

On Jul 8, CM2 = 9.6%, 5.4 pp below the 15% target (RED). CM1 = 61.4% (target 65–70%). Above max, by impact: FC Rent 19.9% (max 18%, +₹1,777; mainly low revenue, amount +10.2% vs normal), Overheads 13.6% (max 12%, +₹1,427; amount normal, low revenue), Packaging 8.2% (max 7%, +₹1,122; amount normal, low revenue), Power & Fuel 13.6% (max 13%, +₹520; mainly low revenue, amount -6.7% vs normal) and 1 more. Revenue was 19.1% below this FC's recent normal while total cost moved -4.8%. Recommend: Costs were normal; revenue fell. Check volumes/billing for the day before cutting costs.

Compare FC-KOL on Jun 20 in the extended data: Manpower 38.0%, with ₹31.5k against a normal ₹21.9k. That one is cause = cost, so the action is "review shift roster, overtime and temp-labour bookings".

7Where the judgment is (and why)

Numbers never from AI

An alert that occasionally invents a number destroys trust. The LLM may only rephrase, and a guard checks every number and line name.

Rank by ₹

With realistic bands most days have some breach. Severity tiers and ₹ ranking keep the digest short.

Revenue vs cost

An exact two-part split against the FC's own normal: "sell more" and "spend less" are different actions.

Missing ≠ zero

A blank cost cell makes the day "Data issue", never a fake Blue day. A real ₹0 is kept but called out.

No gaps in rules

"12–14.9%" leaves 14.95 undefined, so the boundaries are explicit, tested at every edge, and kept in config.

Delivery separate

The analysis is saved first. Sending can fail or retry without losing it, and the outbox always gets a copy.

8How it gets smarter over time

  • Memory (built): past days per FC, used for baselines, 7-day history, repeat offenders, and trends on one-day uploads.
  • Manual overrides (built): explain or snooze. Overridden items leave the digest's top issues, and explanations are recalled the next time the same line breaks.
  • Trend tracking (built): 4-day rises toward a limit and falling CM2 are shown under "Watch" before they breach.
  • Next: learned per-FC bands (human-approved), alert thresholds tuned by which alerts get acted on, action-outcome tracking, weekday and festival seasonality, and retrieval of similar past explanations.

10What to practise for better understanding

  • Work out CM1% and CM2% by hand for Day 31 and Day 39 of the sample, and check them against outputs/provided_sample/pnl_report.xlsx.
  • Change Manpower's max to 31 on the Settings page, re-run the sample, and count how the line-only days change. You'll see how sensitive alerting is to targets.
  • Prove the cause-split identity: A/R − A₀/R₀ = (A−A₀)/R + (A₀/R − A₀/R₀). Then try a day where both cost and revenue moved.
  • Upload test-data/daily_2026-08-08.csv after running the extended sample, and see how memory gives a one-day file a baseline and trend.
  • Mark FC-BLR Overheads on Aug 8 as "explained", then preview the digest again. The issue moves to "Acknowledged".
  • Upload each file in test-data/bad/ and read the messages. Then invent a new bad file and see whether the message is still clear.
  • Run Mailpit, send digests, and open them at localhost:8025. Compare the HTML and text versions.
  • Turn on AI_INSIGHTS, then write a fake LLM reply with a wrong number in server/test/analyst.test.js and watch the guard reject it.
  • Read tools/generate.js: why is the answer key computed with separate arithmetic instead of calling the engine?
  • Add a new header synonym (for example "Security cost" into Overheads) and write a test for it.

11Questions & answers

Why not let an LLM agent read the spreadsheet and decide everything?

It would be slower, cost tokens, give different answers on reruns, and occasionally get arithmetic wrong. Margin alerts must be exact and auditable. The "agentic" part here is the decomposition into stages, each with one job, with the LLM limited to language, where it actually helps.

Why is a Yellow day not in the anomaly log?

The brief flags CM2 only beyond ±3 pp of 15–30%, which means below 12 or above 33. Yellow (12–15 and 30–33) is inside that tolerance, so it's coloured as a warning but isn't a breach. Line items on a Yellow day are still flagged if they break their own band.

What is "₹ impact"?

The rupees by which a line overshot its limit: (pct − max) × revenue. It makes a 1 pp breach at a ₹1.5L FC more important than a 1 pp breach at an ₹80k FC, which is what matters to the business.

How does it know "normal"?

It uses the median of that FC's previous 14 good days, from the same file or from memory of earlier runs. It needs at least 5 days; before that, the cause is reported as unknown.

What happens if a cost cell is blank?

The day becomes a "Data issue" (grey), and margins aren't computed. Treating blank as 0 would invent a high margin, which is exactly the false Blue the system is meant to catch.

How would a non-technical user handle a large file?

They upload it and see a progress bar. They can close the tab, because the run continues on the server. When they come back, the run is listed under "Upload & runs". 18,250 FC-days take about half a second; only the optional AI wording is slow, so it's capped.

Is the email real?

Yes, through SMTP. Mailpit is a free local inbox for testing. Gmail with an app password delivers to real inboxes. Slack webhooks are optional. A copy of every digest is always written to the outbox folder.

Where are the target ranges from?

CM1 and CM2 targets and the colour bands are from the brief. The line-item ranges weren't in the provided export, so placeholders are used and clearly labelled. If the uploaded workbook contains a target table, it's detected and used for that run.

What would you build next?

Per-FC learned bands with human approval, to cut noise; alert thresholds tuned by feedback; tracking whether recommended actions worked; and authentication with roles, so each manager only sees their own FC.
